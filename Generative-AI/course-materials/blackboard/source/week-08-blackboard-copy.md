## Discussion Board

### Assignment 1 — What Makes an AI Claim Credible?

> [SUBMISSION] Type: Discussion Board. Points: 10. Submit one initial post and two peer responses in the Week 8 Discussion item. Use Blackboard’s displayed due dates.

Write an initial post of approximately **350–500 words**. Make a defensible claim, compare alternatives, and use evidence from the assigned material. Connect your judgment to your research-assistant design rather than summarizing the chapter.

#### Discussion Prompt

**Part A — Position:** Choose a specific AI capability claim relevant to your project and identify its original source.

**Part B — Comparison:** Assess the evaluation setup, baseline, source type, and scope of the claimed result. Explain a plausible alternative interpretation.

**Part C — Judgment:** State whether the claim should change your design now, later, or not at all, and identify the missing evidence.

#### Peer Response Guidance

Reply to **two classmates, 150–200 words each**. Challenge an assumption, introduce a counterexample, identify an overlooked trade-off, ask a substantive question, or connect the design to another course concept. Agreement, praise, or summary alone is insufficient. Explain why your contribution matters to the peer’s decision.

## Assignment 2

### Assignment 2 — Benchmark Claim Investigation + Literature Synthesis

> [SUBMISSION] Type: Applied analysis. Points: 25. Submit one PDF or DOCX to Week 8 Assignment 2. Aim for 600–800 words of analysis plus the requested matrix, diagram, prompts, or evidence appendix; these artifacts are not included in the word guide.

Use **How a Wrong Benchmark Claim Spreads** as the anchor. Your task is to explain and test the design reasoning, not retell the case. Work through the following connected steps with informative headings.

#### Detailed Response Prompt

1. Start from How a Wrong Benchmark Claim Spreads. Select one real, traceable claim; do not accuse a source of contamination without evidence.

2. Create a claim-provenance table covering the original report, one independent discussion or evaluation where available, task, dataset, baseline, metric, and limitations. Mark missing evidence explicitly.

3. Compare what the evidence supports with what a headline might imply. Analyze contamination risk, setup mismatch, or baseline weakness as questions to investigate, not assumed findings.

4. Write a thematic synthesis connecting at least three sources to one project decision. Feed the strongest sources and gaps into your 5–8-source midterm proposal.

#### Evidence and Submission Guidance

Use the same assumptions consistently across the response. A design exercise can be completed as a documented walkthrough; do not disguise a projected outcome as an experiment. Where a task asks for live observations, use accessible tools and retain the relevant outputs. If access prevents the required comparison, request an instructor-provided output set rather than inventing results. Explain what your evidence cannot establish.

This week's milestone is your formal midterm checkpoint. Submit:

A strong **Literature Review & Project Proposal** addresses the actual users and constraints from your charter. Where no length is specified, a focused 1–3 pages plus necessary diagrams or evidence is normally sufficient; the literature review may need more space for its 5–8 sources. Identify one uncertainty or decision to revisit. Label any change to a prior milestone so your portfolio remains coherent.
