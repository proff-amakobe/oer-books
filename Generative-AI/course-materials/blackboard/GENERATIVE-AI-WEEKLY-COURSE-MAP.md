## Approved Course Objectives

**CO 1:** Analyze the theoretical foundations, architectures, and underlying mechanisms of Large Language Models and generative artificial intelligence systems.

Maps to PSLOs 1.

**CO 2:** Design and implement applications that leverage Large Language Models and generative AI techniques to solve complex computational problems.

Maps to PSLOs 2, 3.

**CO 3:** Evaluate the performance, capabilities, limitations, and reliability of Large Language Models using appropriate experimental methodologies and evaluation metrics.

Maps to PSLOs 3, 4.

**CO 4:** Assess ethical, societal, legal, and security considerations associated with the development and deployment of generative artificial intelligence systems.

Maps to PSLOs 5.

**CO 5:** Investigate and synthesize advanced methodologies, architectures, emerging technologies, and current research trends in Large Language Models and generative artificial intelligence.

Maps to PSLOs 1, 4, 6.

## Approved Program Student Learning Outcomes

**PSLO 1:** Students will demonstrate advanced knowledge of computer science, including the ability to identify, understand, and apply emerging technologies, tools, and trends relevant to the discipline.

**PSLO 2:** Students will be able to formulate and implement effective solutions to complex and multifaceted problems by applying appropriate computing principles, algorithms, and methodologies.

**PSLO 3:** Students will design, implement, and evaluate software systems to meet desired needs, adhering to standards of quality, scalability, and maintainability.

**PSLO 4:** Students will demonstrate the ability to investigate and synthesize current developments in computer science through critical evaluation of research, literature, and industry advancements.

**PSLO 5:** Students will exhibit a clear understanding of professional, ethical, legal, security, and societal responsibilities in the practice of computing, and apply this knowledge to real-world contexts.

**PSLO 6:** Students will demonstrate depth of expertise in at least one specialized area of computer science, reflecting advanced study and practical application in that domain.

## Approved 1,000-point grading model

| Component | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–15: 10 discussion + 25 applied analysis + 15 milestone per week | 750 |
| Week 16: 10 reflection + 20 integrated portfolio + 20 demonstration | 50 |
| Separate residency assessment | 200 |
| **Course total** | **1,000** |

All sixteen weeks contribute 50 points each: **800 weekly points (80%)**. The separate residency session contributes **200 points (20%)**. Residency is not part of the Week 16 portfolio or demonstration and must have its own Blackboard gradebook item. Its activity and rubric are outside this weekly-guide package; the 200 points are reserved without inventing residency requirements.

This allocation follows the author’s updated instruction and supersedes the original prompt’s default 2,875-point model.
